**What the objective requires.** Recognizing the ring, detecting a pulse, recognizing a tap, and recognizing the person are different claims. The strict biometric objective is to accept the enrolled owner on a later day while rejecting an unfamiliar person wearing that same ring. A useful system also needs acceptable rejection rates, reliable acquisition, and protection against stale or substituted sensor records. A two-person separation is a feasibility observation, not a population false-acceptance estimate.

There is also a practical alternative if an owner-held secret is acceptable: enter a PIN or secret sequence through ring-detected taps. That uses signals from the same ring, but verifies knowledge of a credential rather than uniquely identifying the body. It should be evaluated separately from passive biometrics.

**Published PPG results are mixed for identifiable reasons.** Pulse shape reflects cardiovascular and local tissue properties, but also pressure, temperature, vascular tone, activity, position, filtering, and sensor settings. A classifier can recognize a recording session instead of a person. The important comparison is therefore across days and independent donnings, with unfamiliar impostors and the same physical device.

| Primary study | Published observation | Relevance to Pulse Key |
|---|---|---|
| [Sancho et al., 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5981424/) | Their best 30-cycle configuration averaged about 6.9% equal-error rate in the short-term evaluation and 20.8% in the long-term evaluation | Repeated beats within one session do not establish persistence |
| [Lovisotto et al., *Seeing Red*, 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.07088) | Fifteen people, 6–11 sessions each; approximately 8% best aggregated EER, about 20% across sessions | Finger PPG also suffers session changes; this is not exclusively a wrist problem |
| [Kumar et al., *Fusion-ID*, 2022](https://machinelearning.apple.com/research/photoplethysmography-motion-sensor-fusion) | Multichannel PPG plus motion, 247 users; reported 12% false rejection at 1% false acceptance | Strong reason to investigate complementary modalities; those figures do not transfer to Oura |
| [Alecci et al., 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12736706/) | Replicated methods on longitudinal data from 44 participants; temporal evaluation produced MCC below 0.17 | Strong evidence against relying on random-window validation; not proof every PPG method must fail |
| [Shao et al., *Know Me by My Pulse*](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.13690v1) | Four-channel, 25 Hz PPG; reported FAR 0.48%, FRR 11.77%, EER 2.76% | Promising result whose evaluation design needs close attention |

In the accessible Shao manuscript, four-second windows overlap by 50%, and each participant’s segments are divided approximately 60/20/20 between training, validation, and testing. The description does not establish session-disjoint partitions for the headline result. This creates a leakage concern; it does not prove the implementation leaked. Its study-specific result at 25 Hz is also not a universal physical sampling limit. [Methods and reported results](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.13690v1)

The disagreement between positive and negative papers is therefore not resolved by choosing the newest model. Channel count, sensor geometry, activity coverage, test separation, and the threat model differ. None of these studies validates owner authentication on your particular ring.

**The Library waveform decoder is wrong for the stored JSON format.** The original Rust decoder accumulates delta bytes within each packet, starting each packet at zero unless an absolute marker is present. Consequently, a non-marker packet’s `ppg_samples` values are cumulative offsets within that packet. The Library’s `reconstruct()` treats each of those values as an individual delta and accumulates them again. [Rust packet decoder](/Users/theamazingrayan/Desktop/OuraDeepSeek/ring/crates/oura-protocol/src/events.rs:327), [archived Library reconstruction](/Users/theamazingrayan/Documents/Codex/2026-11-03/you-are-taking-on-an-open/work/phase1/pulse_key_phase1/feature_extraction.py:15)

A small synthetic example demonstrates the distinction. If the previous packet ends at 102, and the next packet contains byte deltas −1 and +3, its stored cumulative offsets are −1 and +2. The correct next samples are 101 and 104. The Library produces 101 and 103. This is an extra accumulation, not merely an arbitrary baseline offset.

I compared two independent correction paths: decoding the continuous raw byte stream, and adding each packet’s stored relative values to the preceding packet’s final sample. They agree exactly for **all nine complete, single-marker historical bursts**. The archived Library reconstruction agrees with neither for any of those nine. A synthetic regression also checks every byte partition of a short stream, including marker boundaries. These checks are in the separate [offline decoder audit](/Users/theamazingrayan/Documents/Codex/2026-11-03/you-are-taking-on-an-open/outputs/offline_decoder_audit.py); no archived module was patched.

Their synthetic reconstruction example assumes the wrong input semantics, and their replay tests preserve the old outcomes. Passing those tests was therefore insufficient evidence of valid measurement. **A useful physical model treats calibration as a measurement of the controller’s response.** A simplified hypothesis is

`measured ADC = offset ± gain × illumination × tissue return + noise`.

If firmware adjusts illumination until the ADC reaches a target, similar final ADC values across people are expected. The adjustment needed to reach that target may retain tissue/contact information. However, illumination code, gain, target, wavelength, path, contact and temperature must be accounted for before that adjustment can be called anatomical.

This explains why regulated optical DC does not automatically eliminate the biometric opportunity, and why control effort might be more informative than the final ADC mean. It also explains a weakness in the reported “perfusion index”: `100 × AC / raw ADC mean` is not necessarily physiological AC/DC when the raw ADC contains an unknown offset, sign convention or cancellation term. Until calibrated, it is an instrument-dependent ratio.

The next representation should preserve the pair `(N3,N5)`, their final control codes, configuration bytes and slot identifiers. Difference and sum retain the pair’s information; adding the difference to the original pair does not create another independent dimension. Candidate log-ratio/common-mode features can be compared, but their physical interpretation requires stable gains and targets. A deterministic ramp whose length is determined by N is not a new modality unless timing, settling or residuals add reproducible information.

Pressure is a concrete confound, not a hypothetical excuse: controlled PPG experiments have measured changes in AC and DC with contact force. [Rafolt and Gallasch, 2004](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15032494/) On this project, the crucial causal test is whether the feature follows the person when day, orientation and fit change, or follows contact conditions when people change.

A promising adjacent field is physics-based inverse optical modeling. Behrmann and colleagues’ PPGen/HAI work links optical signals to tissue parameters and sensor geometry. It is **simulation evidence**, not demonstrated wearer recognition: the paper uses in-silico datasets, assumes simplified geometry, and does not release its internal simulator/data. It also finds some vessel parameters difficult to identify with few wavelengths. Its value here is as a method for testing identifiability and nuisance sensitivity, not as a ready-made finger fingerprint model. [Primary manuscript](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.02073v1)

**The strongest biometric direction is optical calibration plus correctly decoded pulse shape, conditioned on instrument state.** Calibration currently supplies the historical separation; corrected waveform morphology remains a comparator and possible complementary source. Neither should be promoted independently. Start with a small, interpretable model and explicit abstention for unsupported measurement states. A large neural network cannot compensate for two wearers, session confounding or incorrect inputs.

**Security cannot be inferred from a plausible pulse.** Published work has examined attacks using PPG from other body sites, and a separate preprint explores signals recovered from video. Neither establishes a physical attack against this Oura ring, but they rule out assuming that physiological signals are inherently secret or unreplayable. Authentication of the BLE device does not prove the wearer’s identity. A fresh host request does not by itself prove that returned history was freshly sampled. A randomized motion prompt can add evidence of recent participation; it does not itself create person specificity or cryptographic sensor attestation.

**The experimental roadmap should answer the cheapest decisive questions first.** The following collection and deployment steps are proposals. They have not been executed and require the permissions specified in your request. The previously frozen protocol remains intact; a decoder-corrected study needs its own version and preregistration.

1. **Finish instrument validation before collecting more people.** The separate decoder audit already resolves the demonstrated accumulation error and reproduces corrected scores. Next, create a new offline candidate with strict binary tuple parsing, explicit device/firmware/capture identity, duplicate detection, and clear treatment of missing or uncertain records. Keep the historical versions as comparators. Test packet splits, truncation, reordered records, alternate slots, repeated captures and timestamp resets. **Success:** exact raw/decoded agreement on eligible archived bursts and deterministic abstention on malformed or ambiguous fixtures. **Failure:** any unexplained sample discrepancy or acceptance based on missing provenance. Do not silently require monotonic debug order without resolving the observed exception.

2. **Run the planned owner falsification test: five ordinary reseats, then five on another day.** Use the same physical ring and usual finger; settle before calibration starts. Freeze the existing optical `[-5,0]` rule as one comparator, alongside the separately versioned corrected waveform model. Record every attempt, including failures, latency and incomplete data. Deliberate rotations belong in a separately labeled stress arm. **Continue criterion:** at least 9/10 complete accepts under ordinary conditions, used only as an engineering screen. **Failure criterion:** two or more ordinary attempts fail to accept, or a repeated quality-passing positive dN contradicts the optical boundary. Investigate the cause rather than widen the threshold on these test data. Ten successes would still not establish reliability.

3. **Test unfamiliar wearers promptly.** Recruit five consenting people with comfortably compatible fingers; use three independent reseats on each of two days, giving 30 stranger attempts. Interleave owner returns and counterbalance collection order. Keep identity labels hidden from scoring and freeze all thresholds. Record fit, orientation, acquisition state, temperature if available, and pulse rate; do not select easy impostors or discard inconvenient attempts. **Failure of the current no-collision hypothesis:** any clean stranger acceptance. **Continue criterion:** no such acceptance, acceptable owner usability, and enough usable stranger captures to learn something. If over 20% are inconclusive, investigate acquisition before calling the classifier effective. Five strangers are a falsification cohort, not security validation.

4. **Use those observations to choose a model, then obtain a new test cohort.** Compare optical difference alone, the full optical pair with instrument-state covariates, corrected waveform alone, their fusion, and a brief ring-motion challenge. A simple regularized score is preferable initially. Partition by person, day and donning before making windows. Keep final impostor people out of feature selection. **Success:** on untouched data, a candidate reduces false accepts at comparable genuine acceptance and acquisition coverage, with uncertainty reported. **Failure:** the advantage disappears on later days, follows orientation rather than wearer, or depends on post-hoc exclusions. Any model adapted after the first cohort needs new confirmatory data.

5. **Confirm a specified operating point and test security separately.** An illustrative research target is an upper 95% bound below 1% false acceptance, false rejection no more than 10%, and inconclusive attempts no more than 10%, with acquisition latency reported. These are proposed research gates, not a certificate for Mac unlocking. A provisional latency budget of 60 seconds is consistent with a roughly 30-second waveform plus several seconds of calibration and transport; measure it rather than assume it. Test replay, duplicated history, interrupted capture, disconnect/reconnect, sleep, removal/handoff and stale-session reuse in an offline harness first. **Failure:** any stale/ambiguous input grants authorization or continuity survives a known break. Live integration requires a separate reviewed design and your permission.

Record false accepts per all impostor attempts, false matches conditional on usable captures, genuine rejects, genuine inconclusive attempts, and the fraction of impostor people ever accepted. Otherwise a system that abstains almost everywhere can appear secure. Report all attempted acquisitions and predefined retry behavior. Windows, beats and repeated attempts from one person are correlated; they must not be counted as independent people.

For perspective, with zero errors in n genuinely independent Bernoulli trials, the exact one-sided 95% upper bound is `1 − 0.05^(1/n)`. This gives:

| Zero errors observed in | Upper bound under that model |
|---:|---:|
| 5 independent trials | 45.1% |
| 30 | 9.50% |
| 300 | 0.994% |
| 3,000 | 0.0998% |

Zero-error demonstrations require at least 299, 2,995 or 29,956 independent trials for bounds below 1%, 0.1% or 0.01%, respectively. Repeating one easy stranger thousands of times does not justify those population claims. A real study needs enough distinct people and days, with cluster-aware uncertainty. The numbers explain why this project cannot yet claim a useful FAR; they do not prescribe an unnecessarily large first feasibility experiment.

**My recommendation is to continue a bounded biometric investigation, starting with measurement correctness and a same-ring crossover.** Keep calibration as the leading optical hypothesis, restore correctly decoded waveform morphology as a legitimate comparator, and include motion behavior if the collection burden is small. Do not spend the next phase searching many models against the same two people. Do not deploy the current historical optical split.

If normal owner variation overlaps unfamiliar wearers after controlling acquisition state, that rejects the tested representation and operating point. It does not prove every signal from Ring 4 is useless. The next hardware requirement would be documented raw access to multiple optical paths/wavelengths with gain, illumination and timing metadata; potentially a pressure/contact measurement; and a trustworthy fresh-capture path. A configurable research ring such as τ-Ring illustrates the sort of multimodal access needed, but switching hardware would leave the original “same physical Oura” objective and would not itself guarantee biometric success. [τ-Ring primary platform paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.00778)

For a practical authentication product sooner, evaluate a secret entered through the ring, or external strong authentication followed by conservative ring wear continuity. State explicitly which objective each meets. Passive, reliable recognition of this ring’s wearer remains an empirical question blocked by **cross-day owner stability, unfamiliar same-ring wearers, verified instrument-state semantics, and capture freshness**—not by a demonstrated physical impossibility.
